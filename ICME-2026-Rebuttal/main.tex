\noindent\textcolor{blue}{\textbf{$\triangleright$ To All Reviewers / Area Chair:}}

\noindent We sincerely thank all reviewers for their constructive feedback and have carefully revised the manuscript accordingly.

\noindent\textbf{Q1: Concern regarding Incremental Novelty / Engineering Combination} \\

\noindent\textbf{A:} We respectfully clarify that ACME-YOLO is not a mere combination of existing techniques. While basic operators like attention are established, our core contribution is a \textbf{constraint-aware structural co-design} tailored for the extreme accuracy-efficiency trade-off in UAV detection. 

\noindent Instead of naively stacking heavy modules, we resolve mutually coupled bottlenecks via a tightly optimized \textbf{enhancement--fusion--restoration} pipeline:
\noindent Specifically, \textbf{CCRE} embeds iAFF into a dual-path residual structure with a learnable scaling factor $\alpha$, enhancing shallow-layer discriminability for minute objects while strictly bounding parameter bloat. Simultaneously, \textbf{ADRF-Fusion} abandons expensive multi-scale modules in favor of parallel depthwise dilated convolutions with Softmax weighting, achieving adaptive receptive field fusion with near-zero overhead. As proven in ablation studies, rather than being isolated add-ons, these components form a highly synergistic closed-loop.

\noindent Therefore, our novelty is not claimed at the basic operator level, but rather in establishing a \textbf{lightweight architectural paradigm} that successfully bridges the gap between high-precision small-object feature recovery and stringent edge-deployment efficiency.

\noindent\textcolor{rev8color}{\textbf{$\triangleright$ Reviewer\#8.}}

\noindent\textbf{Q1: Concern on the rationality of "Adaptive" in ADRF}\\
\noindent\textbf{A:} We respectfully clarify that "Adaptive" denotes \textbf{data-driven, task-level adaptation} rather than costly instance-wise gating. During training, the Softmax-normalized weights automatically adapt to the UAV dataset's scale distribution, prioritizing optimal receptive fields for small objects without heavy computational overhead of dynamic attention.

\noindent\textbf{Q2: Concern on missing FPS/latency and deployment results}\\
\noindent\textbf{A:} The native PyTorch inference speed on an A100 GPU is reported in \cref{tab:across_arch} (182 FPS). We are currently finalizing the TensorRT/ONNX deployment pipelines and will update the comprehensive edge-hardware latency in the final version.

\noindent\textbf{Q3: Concern on insufficient small-object evaluation metrics}\\
\noindent\textbf{A:} As presented in \cref{tab:tide_nano}, we have provided APs (small-object AP) to evaluate small object detection performance, together with TIDE analysis.

\noindent\textbf{Q4: Concern on inconsistent training settings among models}\\
\noindent\textbf{A:}  All compared variants are trained with the same training strategy, input size, and augmentations till convergence.

\noindent\textbf{Q1: Concern on selective metric reporting ($mAP_{50}$ vs. $mAP_{50:95}$)}\\
\noindent\textbf{A:} We clarify that $mAP_{50}$ is the standard metric for ultra-dense datasets like VisDrone. High IoU thresholds overly penalize inherent bounding-box ambiguities via matching noise rather than true misdetections. Regardless, ACME-YOLO still improves $mAP_{50:95}$, and the newly added $AP_S$ (\cref{tab:tide_nano}) explicitly confirms robust gains on tiny targets.

\noindent\textbf{Q2: Concern on missing deep analysis and failure modes}\\
\noindent\textbf{A:}  TIDE analysis (\cref{tab:tide_nano}) mathematically details \textit{why} our modules work:
\textbf{(1) Loc Error} drops, proving AdaptiveUpSample preserves spatial details.
\textbf{(2) Miss \& FalseNeg Errors} decrease, showing CCRE and LDRF-Fusion effectively extract weak features.
Consequently, $AP_S$ improves by +1.15, explicitly resolving the targeted UAV bottlenecks.
